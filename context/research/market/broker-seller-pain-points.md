# Vietnam broker and seller pain points

## Executive finding

`INFERENCE`: Vietnam's central brokerage problem is not only buyer demand. The market lacks a broadly adopted mechanism for proving representation, assigning responsibility for a Listing, recording introductions and showings, governing cooperation, and distinguishing source data from copied advertisements.

This creates a reinforcing loop: uncertain rights encourage brokers to hide information; hidden information fragments data; fragmented data lowers trust; low trust encourages open, weakly governed distribution; and weak accountability reduces investment in Listing quality.

## Broker pain points

- `INFERENCE`: Source and client relationships are difficult to protect when addresses, media, and contacts can be copied across channels.
- `SOURCE CLAIM`: Vietnam market research describes fragmented secondary-market brokerage and weak access to reliable closed-price and property-condition data.
- `INFERENCE`: Listings can remain duplicated, differently priced, or stale because no shared lifecycle owner is accountable.
- `INFERENCE`: Commission and co-broker expectations are often harder to prove without explicit representation, introduction, showing, and cooperation records.
- `INFERENCE`: Legal verification, pricing, lead qualification, and transaction coordination are distributed across chat, social media, spreadsheets, and paper records.

## Owner/Seller pain points

- `SOURCE CLAIM`: Vietnamese reporting documents unauthorized or misleading property advertisements and inconsistent asking prices.
- `INFERENCE`: Owners lack a reliable way to identify the responsible broker, correct copied data, control distribution, or compare asking price with closed evidence.
- `INFERENCE`: Giving one property to many brokers may expand reach but fragments accountability for verification, media, showing feedback, negotiation, and removal of stale advertisements.
- `FACT`: Vietnam's Law on Real Estate Business 29/2023/QH15 requires individual brokers to hold a certificate and work through an eligible real-estate service enterprise; it also requires truthful information and recognizes contractual remuneration and compensation rights. The law does not itself create a national MLS, closed-comparable dataset, or automated commission-protection mechanism.

## U.S. comparison boundary

`INFERENCE`: Written representation, MLS cooperation rules, listing responsibility, and more structured closing infrastructure reduce some forms of bypass risk in the United States. The dominant difficulties shift toward affordability, operating cost, disclosure, inspection, appraisal, financing, insurance, cybersecurity, and failed closings. These mechanisms cannot be copied into Vietnam without legal and operating validation.

## Product opportunities

All items are `PROPOSAL` until approved:

1. Versioned Representation and Listing Agreement records.
2. Seller-controlled distribution consent and correction/withdrawal cases.
3. Introduction, showing, and cooperation events with bounded visibility.
4. Duplicate detection, provenance, source confidence, and accountable lifecycle ownership.
5. Human-reviewed CMA using closed or otherwise qualified evidence.
6. A transaction coordination record distinct from Property, Listing, and billing transactions.

## Open questions

- `OPEN QUESTION`: Which representation and co-broker agreements are enforceable and operationally adoptable in the first Vietnam market?
- `OPEN QUESTION`: Which Listing and showing events may be used to resolve commission disputes?
- `OPEN QUESTION`: Who may see exact owner identity, address, showing, offer, and compensation data?
- `OPEN QUESTION`: Which public or partner sources can lawfully support closed-price evidence?
